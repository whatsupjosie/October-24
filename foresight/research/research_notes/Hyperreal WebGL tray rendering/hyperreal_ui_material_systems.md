# Hyper-real UI material systems: how shipping products and web libraries build "elevated reality" depth, and what transfers to a WebGL2 + HTML-overlay tray UI (Foresight Counter)

Research date: 2026-10-13. Scope: Apple Liquid Glass, Windows Fluent Acrylic/Mica, visionOS glass, product-configurator lighting, three.js MeshPhysicalMaterial and pmndrs drei, HTML-into-WebGL (HTML-in-Canvas), CSS/SVG lensing, accessibility guardrails.
Method note: Several primary pages (three.js shader-chunk source, drei source file, WICG explainer, kube.fm SVG article, Filament docs, WCAG text) could not be fetched in this session because the fetch tool refused URLs not surfaced by search. Where a claim depends on those, it is placed in Gaps or labelled as unverified prior knowledge.

## 1. Apple Liquid Glass (WWDC 2025, iOS/macOS 26): what the material does, Apple's guidance, and open-source web recreations

### Takeaway
Apple describes Liquid Glass as a real-time "lensing" material: it bends and concentrates the light behind it, has highlights that move with light sources and device motion, glows from the touch point, adapts its tint, shadow and light/dark state to what is behind it, and must stay in a floating navigation layer (never glass on glass). Web recreations split into two families: WebGL shaders that sample a rasterized snapshot of the page (liquidGL, @ybouane/liquidglass) and Chromium-only SVG `feDisplacementMap` inside `backdrop-filter`. Neither can refract a live cross-origin iframe.

### Cited Findings
Apple's description of the material ("Meet Liquid Glass", WWDC25 session 219):
- Lensing is "the primary way Liquid Glass visually defines itself". The material "dynamically bends, shapes, and concentrates light in real time", in contrast to earlier materials where light was "scattered". — [WWDC25 Meet Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Size changes the optics: larger elements show "more pronounced lensing and refraction effects, and a softer scattering of light", and they cast "deeper, richer shadows" to simulate a thicker material. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Specular: "Light sources inside of this environment shine on the material producing highlights that respond to geometry". During lock and unlock "these lights move in space, causing light to travel around the material, defining its silhouette". "In some cases, the lighting responds to device motion". — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Touch: "the material illuminates from within". The glow starts "right under your fingertips", "spreads throughout the element and onto any Liquid Glass elements nearby", and the material has "an inherent gel-like flexibility", "flexing and energizing with light". — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Adaptive shadow: the shadow becomes more opaque over text and less opaque over a solid light background. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Adaptive layers: "each layer continuously adapts based on what's behind it". "The amount of tint and the dynamic range shift to always ensure buttons remain legible." Small elements (tab bars) flip between light and dark with the background. Large elements (menus, sidebars) do not flip, because the transition would be distracting. Glyphs flip opposite to the glass to maximize contrast. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Tinting: one chosen color "generates a range of tones that are mapped to content brightness underneath". This imitates real colored glass, changing "hue, brightness and saturation depending on what's behind without deviating too much from the intended color". Tint should be reserved for primary elements and actions. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Two variants that "should never be mixed". Regular is adaptive and legible over anything. Clear is "permanently more transparent", has no adaptive behavior, and "needs a dimming layer". Use Clear only over media-rich content, when a dimming layer won't harm the content, and when the content on top is bold and bright. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Layering: glass belongs in the navigation layer floating above content. "Always avoid glass on glass." Elements placed on glass should use "fills, transparency, and vibrancy" so they read as a thin overlay that is part of the material. In steady states, avoid intersections between content and glass. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Scroll edge effect: as content scrolls under glass, it "gently dissolves the content into the background, lifting the glass visually above the moving content". It switches to a subtle dimming over dark content. A "hard" style is available for extra separation. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- The session transcript contains no shader, blur-radius or IOR values. Implementation details are pushed to "Adopting Liquid Glass" and the HIG. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)

Open-source web recreations:
- **liquidGL** (naughtyduk, MIT, "no runtime dependencies"). It rasterizes the page with its own built-in rasterizer (default target `body`, `resolution` 2.0, clamped 0.1 to 3.0). It renders through WebGPU, then WebGL2, then WebGL1, then CSS `backdrop-filter` as fallbacks. All lenses on a page share one canvas, and the README reports up to 30 elements tested without problems. — [liquidGL GitHub](https://github.com/naughtyduk/liquidGL)
- liquidGL optical parameters: `refraction` 0.01 (base offset across the pane); `bevelDepth` 0.08 (extra refraction at the edge); `bevelWidth` 0.15 (bevel zone as a fraction of the shortest side); `aberration` 0 (R/B dispersion that scales with the refraction offset, so it is strongest at the bevel); `specular` true (animated highlights, slightly more GPU); `frost` (blur radius in px); `magnify` 1. — [liquidGL GitHub](https://github.com/naughtyduk/liquidGL)
- liquidGL motion and shadow: `tilt` gives 3D tilt on cursor movement, with `tiltFactor` 5 degrees (0 to 25 recommended) and `tiltEase` 400 ms settle. `shadow` is a soft drop shadow placed at z-index minus 2. — [liquidGL GitHub](https://github.com/naughtyduk/liquidGL)
- liquidGL limitations: changing elements must be registered with `registerDynamic()`. Video is auto-detected. CSS animations are not supported. Fixed-position elements are ignored. Cross-origin images need CORS headers. All targets must share one effective z-index. Long documents can exceed GPU texture limits. Safari "may be unstable" when a lens covers more than 50% of viewport width or height. The README does not address iframes. — [liquidGL GitHub](https://github.com/naughtyduk/liquidGL)
- **@ybouane/liquidglass** (MIT). It captures DOM with `html-to-image`: it clones subtrees, inlines computed styles and renders through SVG `foreignObject`. `img`, `canvas` and `video` are drawn directly. Requires WebGL 1. — [liquidglass README v1.0.3](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@ybouane/liquidglass@1.0.3/README.md)
- liquidglass defaults: `refraction` 0.69, `chromAberration` 0.05, `edgeHighlight` 0.05, `specular` 0.0 (multi-light Blinn-Phong), `fresnel` 1.0, `blurAmount` 0, `distortion` 0, `cornerRadius` 65 / `zRadius` 40 (shape and bevel depth), `shadowOpacity` 0.30, `shadowSpread` 10, `shadowOffsetY` 1, `bevelMode` 0 (biconvex pill) or 1 (dome). — [liquidglass README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@ybouane/liquidglass@1.0.3/README.md)
- liquidglass costs: each instance opens its own WebGL context, and browsers typically cap contexts at about 16. `init()` takes 100 to 500 ms. Dynamic elements and video re-capture every frame. Resize re-captures everything. Webfonts must be loaded and CORS-served before `init()`. Nested glass is rejected. A tainted canvas disables the effect for the whole root. — [liquidglass README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@ybouane/liquidglass@1.0.3/README.md)
- **CSS + SVG approach** (LogRocket, Dec 8 2025, modified Mar 27 2026). The filter chain is `feImage` (a displacement-map PNG and a specular-rim PNG exported from Figma), then `feDisplacementMap` (scale 55, R/G channel selectors), then `feColorMatrix saturate` 50, then `feComposite in`, then `feBlend`, plus a light `feGaussianBlur` (stdDeviation 1). It is applied with `backdrop-filter: url(#filter) brightness(150%)`. The rim highlight is a hand-drawn PNG rather than `feSpecularLighting`. — [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/how-create-liquid-glass-effects-css-and-svg/)
- "Safari and Firefox do not support this combination yet": only Chromium accepts SVG filters as `backdrop-filter` inputs. The article recommends feature detection with a static gradient or blur fallback, limiting the effect to a few floating elements, and notes that many instances or scrolling content cause jank. — [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/how-create-liquid-glass-effects-css-and-svg/)
- Other recreations surfaced by search but not reviewed: liquid-glass-js (dashersw), RaymonDev/liquidweb, archisvaze/liquid-glass, and the ekino-france Medium write-up on CSS+SVG. — [liquid-glass-js README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/liquid-glass-js@0.1.0/README.md); [liquidweb](https://github.com/RaymonDev/liquidweb); [ekino Medium](https://medium.com/ekino-france/liquid-glass-in-css-and-svg-839985fcb88d)

### Inferences
- Both web libraries refract a snapshot, not live pixels, because WebGL cannot read composited page pixels. Foresight owns the pixels behind each tray (bench, other trays, shadows) because its own WebGL2 renderer draws them. The renderer can therefore do true screen-space refraction at the rim for free: render the bench and scene to a color texture, then in the tray pass offset the UV by a normal derived from the rounded-polygon SDF gradient. No html2canvas or html-to-image step is needed for the bench.
- The liquidGL parameter model (base refraction, plus extra bevelDepth inside a bevelWidth band, plus aberration scaled by the same offset) maps directly to a GLSL rim function: `h = profile(clamp(sdf/bevelWidth,0,1))`, `n = normalize(vec3(-grad(sdf)*dh, 1))`, `uvOffset = n.xy*(refraction + bevelDepth*(1-t))`, with R/G/B sampled at offsets ×(1∓aberration). Values to start from: bevelWidth ≈0.15 of the short side, bevelDepth ≈0.08, aberration small.
- Apple's behaviors that fit the "tray" brief and are cheap to emulate: (a) a highlight that travels around the silhouette when a light moves, driven by pointer position on desktop where Apple uses device motion; (b) "illuminates from within" on press, which suits the "selected tray is backlit" requirement, as an emissive inner glow from the contact point that spreads and bleeds onto neighbours; (c) larger trays cast deeper, softer shadows; (d) shadow opacity raised when a tray sits over busy content.
- Apple's "avoid glass on glass" and "never mix variants" rules argue for one glass recipe per tray (rim glass), with content behind it treated as a "screen", rather than stacking translucent panels inside trays.
- Avoid the per-instance WebGL-context design of @ybouane/liquidglass (16-context cap). Use liquidGL's pattern of one shared canvas for all trays.
- The SVG displacement route is Chromium-only and adds compositor cost. With a WebGL backdrop already in place, it is only worth it for DOM-level lensing over HTML that WebGL cannot see, and only as a progressive enhancement.

### Gaps
- Apple's "Adopting Liquid Glass" article and the current HIG Materials page were not fetched. No numeric parameters (blur, IOR, highlight intensity) are published in the session transcript, and I found no official source giving them.
- Bundle sizes for liquidGL and @ybouane/liquidglass (plus html-to-image) are not stated in their READMEs. Measure from npm/jsDelivr before considering them.
- No independent benchmark of these libraries on integrated GPUs was found.
- The kube.fm article ("Liquid Glass in the browser: refraction with CSS and SVG") reportedly derives displacement maps from Snell's law with surface profiles such as a convex squircle. It could not be fetched, so that is unverified.

## 2. Windows 11 Mica/Acrylic and visionOS glass: blur, noise, luminosity, tint, specular rim

### Takeaway
Fluent Acrylic is a fixed layer recipe: background, blur, exclusion blend, tint overlay, noise. It falls back to a solid color under High Contrast, transparency-off, Battery Saver and low-end hardware. visionOS glass adds environmental lighting (specular highlights and shadows tied to the room), vibrancy for foreground text, and heavier type weights for legibility on glass.

### Cited Findings
- Acrylic recipe in order: background (desktop/other windows for background acrylic, in-app content for in-app acrylic), then blur, then an exclusion blend "that keeps UI placed on the acrylic readable", then a color/tint overlay, then noise "adds texture". — [Microsoft Learn: Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/uwp/style/acrylic)
- Acrylic usage: background acrylic is for transient surfaces (menus, flyouts, light-dismiss panes). In-app acrylic is for supporting UI that overlaps scrolling content. Use opaque backgrounds for vertical panes that section content. Avoid stacking acrylic, and avoid edge-to-edge acrylic panes because they create a visible seam. — [Microsoft Learn: Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/uwp/style/acrylic)
- Acrylic legibility: accent-colored text at the default 14px is likely to fail minimum contrast on acrylic. Customizing tint color or opacity affects legibility. — [Microsoft Learn: Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/uwp/style/acrylic)
- Acrylic fallbacks: High Contrast shows the user's chosen background color. Acrylic becomes a solid color when "Transparency effects" is off, when Battery Saver is on, and on low-end hardware. Background acrylic also goes solid when its window deactivates. — [Microsoft Learn: Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/uwp/style/acrylic)
- Custom acrylic and Mica brushes are documented under "Apply Mica or Acrylic materials in desktop apps for Windows 11". — [Microsoft Learn: Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/uwp/style/acrylic) (link target not fetched)
- visionOS glass lets light from the surroundings and virtual content show through. "Specular highlights and shadows reinforce the window's scale and position in the room." It adjusts contrast and color balance with the lighting (day to night). It has no separate light or dark appearance. — [WWDC23 Design for spatial user interfaces](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/)
- visionOS material hierarchy: avoid opaque windows because they feel "heavy". Use a darker material to separate sections or raise contrast for inputs, and a lighter material to draw attention to interactive elements. Don't stack lighter materials. — [WWDC23 10076](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/)
- Vibrancy "brightens foreground content ... by pulling light and color forward from what's behind it", updated in real time, with primary and secondary levels. Text defaults to white on glass. Prefer white glyphs, and put color on a background layer or a whole button rather than on thin text. — [WWDC23 10076](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/)
- visionOS type is heavier than iOS for contrast on vibrant materials: body Regular becomes Medium, titles Semibold become Bold, with slightly increased tracking. — [WWDC23 10076](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/)
- Depth cues: when layered app-icon art is matched, the system applies a glass layer "with depth, specular highlights, and shadows". Ornaments overlap the window edge by 20 pt. Sheets push the parent back and dim it. Every interactive element gets a hover brightening, with about 4 pt between list items so hover effects don't overlap. Nested corner radius = inner radius + padding. — [WWDC23 10076](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/)
- The visionOS transcript gives no blur radius, opacity, shadow or z-offset values. — [WWDC23 10076](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/)

### Inferences
- The Acrylic recipe is a cheap, proven fallback tier for Foresight when the full physical rim shader is too expensive or the user prefers reduced transparency. Implement it in one fragment pass over the bench texture: downsampled blur, then tint mix, then a tiled noise texture (a few KB) to kill banding. A solid fallback should exist exactly where Windows uses one.
- "Exclusion blend keeps UI readable" and visionOS "vibrancy" both pull foreground contrast from the backdrop. For HTML text over glass, the practical web equivalent is a computed luminance of the backdrop region (readable from the WebGL bench render with a small `readPixels` of a downsampled buffer) used to flip text and glyph color, as Liquid Glass does.
- visionOS's "nested radius = inner radius + padding" rule applies directly to the tray bezel: the content opening radius plus bezel thickness gives the outer rim radius, which keeps the rim concentric and "machined".
- visionOS's environment-tied specular and shadow is the closest shipping analogue to "frames on a bench under lantern light": one consistent key light that every tray's highlight and shadow agree with is what sells physical placement.

### Gaps
- Mica specifics (wallpaper-sampled tint, luminosity layer, Mica Alt) and the AcrylicBrush parameter names and defaults (TintColor, TintOpacity, TintLuminosityOpacity, FallbackColor) were not verified this session. The Fluent 2 material page and the AcrylicBrush API reference were surfaced but not fetched: [Fluent 2 Material](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/material), [AcrylicBrush](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/uwp/api/microsoft.ui.xaml.media.acrylicbrush).
- A dedicated "visionOS rim light / specular edge" spec with numbers was not found. Apple describes the effect only qualitatively.

## 3. Game UI and luxury product configurators: making metal, glass and gems read as premium in real time

### Takeaway
The reliable recipe is image-based lighting from a controlled studio environment, ideally built from emissive light shapes (softboxes and strips) instead of a found HDRI, plus PBR materials, plus restrained post. Reflections of bright strip lights on curved metal and glass are what read as "premium". The sources found are thinner than for the other questions.

### Cited Findings
- three.js / drei **Lightformer** "allows you to create an HDR in realtime using specific shapes that emit light in the scene itself". Shapes mounted in the environment reflect and emit light, at what the poster called no extra cost. — [Babylon.js forum: equivalent to Lightformer](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/babylonjs-equivalent-to-three-js-lightformer/55176)
- The Babylon equivalent recommended there is a custom studio HDR: render a spherical camera at world center in a DCC tool, with modeled light heads and stands in a simple room and a table. Iterate with Radiance `.hdr` (prefiltered on load) and ship Babylon's `.env` format, which "loads fastest". Babylon's default sandbox environment is built this way. — [Babylon.js forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/babylonjs-equivalent-to-three-js-lightformer/55176)
- Babylon reflection probes generate cube maps dynamically but need six renders per frame, so use them with caution, and per a linked thread they do not drive lighting. Lights can be made shadow-only by culling them from meshes, so the environment provides most lighting. — [Babylon.js forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/babylonjs-equivalent-to-three-js-lightformer/55176)
- three.js MeshPhysicalMaterial docs: "For best results, always specify an environment map." — [three.js docs: MeshPhysicalMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)

### Inferences
- For Foresight's bandwidth limits, Lightformer's idea (environment = a few emissive rectangles) can be reproduced with zero download: at startup, render a small procedural cube or equirect map in a shader (warm "lantern" softbox, two long strip lights, dark falloff), then prefilter a handful of roughness mips. This replaces a multi-megabyte HDRI and gives art-directable highlights. Rose gold, gold and silver rims mainly need sharp strip reflections that slide as the pointer moves.
- Polished marble and walnut benches read as premium through a clearcoat lobe (sharp, low-roughness reflection of the same strip lights) over a diffuse base texture. Pearl reads through iridescence plus sheen (section 4).
- Common configurator post (restrained bloom on highlights, ACES or AgX-style tone mapping, subtle vignette) is standard practice. I did not retrieve a citable source for specific configurator post stacks in this session.

### Gaps
- No primary case study (Porsche, Rolex or other car/watch configurators, PlayCanvas, Unreal UMG) with documented lighting rigs or numbers was retrieved. The PlayCanvas forum thread "Lighting in a product configurator" was surfaced but not fetched: [PlayCanvas forum](https://forum.playcanvas.com/t/lighting-in-a-product-configurator/23322).
- Physically based F0 (base reflectance) values for gold, rose gold and silver were not verified this session. Commonly cited linear-RGB F0 values from prior knowledge (Filament / Real-Time Rendering tables) are roughly gold (1.00, 0.77, 0.34) and silver (0.97, 0.96, 0.92). Verify these against the Filament material documentation before use. No authoritative rose-gold F0 was found.
- Asset sizes of typical studio HDRIs (Poly Haven 1k/2k .hdr) were not measured.

## 4. three.js MeshPhysicalMaterial features and the pmndrs ecosystem: what to borrow as GLSL

### Takeaway
MeshPhysicalMaterial exposes a complete layered-BSDF feature set (clearcoat, transmission with thickness and Beer-Lambert attenuation, IOR, dispersion, thin-film iridescence, sheen, anisotropy, specular tint). drei's MeshTransmissionMaterial adds a cheaper, art-directable refraction path: a dedicated low-resolution scene buffer, multi-sample chromatic aberration and noise blur. For a hand-written WebGL2 renderer, the highest-value borrowings are clearcoat, transmission+attenuation, iridescence, anisotropy and MTM's buffer-sampling approach.

### Cited Findings
MeshPhysicalMaterial properties and defaults:
- `anisotropy` 0 (0 to 1, "brushed-metal-style highlights"). `anisotropyRotation` is documented as 1 on the fetched page, which is likely a doc error; usually 0, so verify. `anisotropyMap`: RG = direction in tangent/bitangent space [-1,1], B = strength. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
- `clearcoat` 0, `clearcoatRoughness` 0, and `clearcoatNormalMap` (the clear layer gets its own normals; `clearcoatNormalScale` (1,1)). — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
- `transmission` 0 (set `opacity` to 1 when used). `thickness` 0 means thin-walled; otherwise the surface is a volume boundary. `attenuationColor` (1,1,1) is "the color white light turns into due to absorption when it reaches attenuationDistance". `attenuationDistance` Infinity. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
- `ior` 1.5 (range 1.0 to 2.333). `reflectivity` 0.5 (equals IOR 1.5, no effect at metalness 1). `specularIntensity` 1 and `specularColor` (1,1,1) tint F0 for non-metals only. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
- `dispersion` 0: "angular color separation (chromatic aberration) through a clear volume", typical range 0 to 1, transmissive objects only. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
- `iridescence` 0 (soap bubbles, oil films). `iridescenceIOR` 1.3. `iridescenceThicknessRange` [100, 400]. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
- `sheen` 0, `sheenColor` (0,0,0), `sheenRoughness` 1, for cloth and fabric. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
- Cost: MeshPhysicalMaterial has higher per-pixel cost than other three.js materials, and each enabled effect adds cost. — [three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)

drei MeshTransmissionMaterial (MTM):
- It accepts all physical-material props plus `chromaticAberration` 0.03, `anisotropicBlur` 0.1, `distortion` 0, `distortionScale` 0.5, `temporalDistortion` 0, `samples` 6, `resolution` (fullscreen by default), `backside` false, `backsideThickness` 0, `backsideResolution`, `buffer`, `transmissionSampler` false, `background`. `roughness` acts as blur. — [drei docs: MeshTransmissionMaterial](https://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-transmission-material)
- Mechanism: it renders the scene into a buffer in an extra pass with the host mesh temporarily removed, then samples that buffer for refraction. It can therefore "see" other transmissive or transparent objects, which MeshPhysicalMaterial transmission cannot. — [drei docs](https://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-transmission-material)
- Performance: lower `samples` and `resolution` for speed. With `roughness`, "a very small buffer (for example, 32x32) still looks good and runs much faster". Share one `buffer` across meshes to avoid one scene render per material. `backside` costs more but looks better. The docs describe it as typically cheaper than MeshPhysicalMaterial's transmission. — [drei docs](https://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-transmission-material)
- Related tutorial (Codrops, warping 3D text inside a glass torus with MTM) was surfaced but not reviewed. — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=89382)

### Inferences
Mapping to Foresight materials (hand-written GLSL, WebGL2):
- **Metal rims (gold, rose gold, silver):** GGX specular with a colored F0 and metalness 1, IBL from the procedural studio environment, plus `anisotropy` (tangent along the rim path) for a brushed or turned-metal look on silver. An optional `clearcoat` lobe gives a lacquered, jewelry-polish double highlight.
- **Jewel-toned glass panels:** transmission with `thickness` and Beer-Lambert `attenuationColor`/`attenuationDistance`. Color deepens with thickness, so bevels look richer and darker than the center, which is the "jewel" cue. Add small `dispersion` or MTM-style `chromaticAberration` only at the bevel. Rim glow comes from a Fresnel term plus the edge-highlight band.
- **Pearl inlays:** thin-film `iridescence` (IOR ≈1.3, thickness 100 to 400 nm range) over a soft white dielectric with `sheen`. This is cheap, analytic, and needs no textures.
- **Polished marble / violet stone bench:** diffuse albedo texture (or procedural veining) plus `clearcoat` 1 with low `clearcoatRoughness`, and optionally a `clearcoatNormalMap` for subtle polish waviness.
- **Refraction buffer strategy:** follow MTM rather than MeshPhysicalMaterial. Render the bench and scene once per frame into one shared, downsampled buffer (half-res, or much lower when frosted). All trays sample it, with `samples` of about 3 to 6 on an integrated GPU. This matches drei's advice and liquidGL's single shared canvas.
- **Source files to read when porting** (paths from prior knowledge of the three.js repo, not fetched this session, so verify): `src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js` (GGX, clearcoat, sheen, iridescence and anisotropy BRDF code), `transmission_pars_fragment.glsl.js` (volume transmission sampling, Beer-Lambert, roughness-to-mip), and drei `src/core/MeshTransmissionMaterial.tsx` (the shader patches for multi-sample chromatic aberration).

### Gaps
- The three.js shader-chunk sources and the drei MTM source file could not be fetched (tool provenance restriction). Exact GLSL, including how dispersion and anisotropy are evaluated and MTM's sampling loop, is unverified here.
- pmndrs/postprocessing (bloom, tone mapping, SMAA) and its bundle size were not researched in this session.
- Download sizes of three.js, drei and postprocessing were not measured. If Foresight stays hand-written, these are reference code only, with no bandwidth cost.

## 5. Making HTML content feel inside a physical object: HTML-in-Canvas, CSS over a WebGL backdrop, iframes, parallax and specular

### Takeaway
As of October 2026, the only way to get live DOM into a WebGL texture with interactivity is Chrome's HTML-in-Canvas API. It is still an origin trial, extended through Chrome 160, with API names that changed in Chrome 150 and 155, and cross-origin iframes are explicitly not supported. Since Foresight trays host iframes, the robust architecture keeps HTML/iframes as real DOM layered between WebGL passes. The "screen behind a bezel" illusion then comes from the frame, shadows and reflective highlights drawn around and over the content, not from refracting the content.

### Cited Findings
- HTML-in-Canvas origin trial was announced for Chrome 148 to 150. Local testing uses Canary 149+ with `chrome://flags/#canvas-draw-element`. — [Chrome blog: HTML-in-Canvas origin trial](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas-origin-trial?hl=de)
- A later update (page last updated 2026-09-29) says the origin trial "runs through Chrome 160 and has been extended", and that tokens must be renewed. — [Chrome blog: HTML-in-Canvas updates](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas-ot-changes). These two posts are consistent once the extension is accounted for. Early-2026 tutorials will use outdated names.
- Current API (Chrome 155+): 2D `ctx.drawElementImage(element, x, y)`, which now returns void, and syncs hit-testing and focus rings automatically. WebGL `texElementSubImage2D` (renamed from `texElementImage2D`) adds `xoffset`/`yoffset` and requires storage pre-allocated with `texImage2D`. WebGPU `drawElementImageToTexture` (renamed from `copyElementImageToTexture`) takes `sourceDict`/`destDict`. The canvas attribute `layoutsubtree` became `content="drawable"`, with a `drawable` opt-in on descendants. Other pieces: `canvas.captureElementImage(element)`, `canvas.updateElementGeometry(element, { canvasTransform })`, and a `paint` event to trigger updates. — [Chrome blog: HTML-in-Canvas updates](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas-ot-changes)
- In WebGL/WebGPU contexts, developers must build a `DOMMatrix` and call `updateElementGeometry()` so pointer events and accessibility map to the 3D-placed element. The earlier API used `getElementTransform()` from an MVP matrix. — [Chrome blog: updates](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas-ot-changes); [Chrome blog: origin trial](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas-origin-trial?hl=de)
- "Cross-origin iframe content isn't supported, for security and privacy reasons." The page does not say whether same-origin iframes can be drawn. Embedded content stays in the accessibility tree. — [Chrome blog: origin trial](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas-origin-trial?hl=de)
- Performance caveat: rendering runs on the main thread in JavaScript, so scrolling and animations inside canvas content cannot update independently as normal composited DOM does. Size the backing store to devicePixelRatio with ResizeObserver to avoid blur. — [Chrome blog: origin trial](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas-origin-trial?hl=de)
- three.js and PlayCanvas have experimental support. — [Chrome blog: origin trial](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/html-in-canvas-origin-trial?hl=de)
- Microsoft Edge lists an HTML-in-Canvas origin trial entry. — [Edge origin trials](https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/origin-trials/trials/a297467e-0030-4c4c-8739-48e130026c03) (surfaced, not fetched)
- Snapshot-based alternatives (html-to-image via SVG `foreignObject`, or custom rasterizers) need CORS-clean images and fonts, re-capture on change, and cost 100 to 500 ms to initialize. — [liquidglass README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@ybouane/liquidglass@1.0.3/README.md); [liquidGL](https://github.com/naughtyduk/liquidGL)
- SVG-filter lensing via `backdrop-filter: url(#f)` works only in Chromium. Safari and Firefox accept only built-in filter functions in `backdrop-filter`. — [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/how-create-liquid-glass-effects-css-and-svg/)
- Pointer-driven tilt reference values: 5 degrees default, 0 to 25 recommended, 400 ms ease. — [liquidGL](https://github.com/naughtyduk/liquidGL)

### Inferences
- **Recommended layer stack per tray** (works in all browsers, keeps iframes live and interactive):
  1. A WebGL2 canvas below, drawing the bench, all tray frames and rims, contact and drop shadows, the backlight glow of the selected tray, and the frame-opening "cavity" (an inner-shadow gradient inside the opening).
  2. DOM content: an iframe or div positioned and clipped to the tray opening with CSS `transform` matching the projected quad, and `clip-path: path()` matching the rounded irregular polygon.
  3. A gloss layer above, with `pointer-events: none`: either a second WebGL canvas or per-tray CSS pseudo-elements carrying (a) an inner shadow at the bezel edge so the screen looks recessed, (b) a soft diagonal reflection gradient with `mix-blend-mode: screen` (or `plus-lighter`) whose position follows the pointer and key light, and (c) a thin bright rim line where glass meets metal.
  
  This mirrors Apple's guidance that glass floats above content and content stays legible beneath it.
- WebGL cannot sample iframe pixels, and HTML-in-Canvas excludes cross-origin iframes. So refraction or blur of tray content itself is limited to same-origin, non-iframe HTML in Chrome with the origin-trial token. Treat it as a future enhancement, never the baseline.
- A single WebGL context cannot sit both below and above DOM. Two canvases means two contexts, or the top layer is CSS-only. CSS-only highlights are cheap and keep the context count at one. Keep the top layer additive (screen/lighten blends with low alpha) so it never reduces text contrast much.
- When trays flex against each other, deform the WebGL frame geometry. The DOM content can follow only with affine or 3D transforms, not arbitrary bends. Keep the opening's deformation subtle, or draw the bezel lip slightly over the content edge so small mismatches are hidden.
- Pointer-driven specular: drive one shared light direction uniform from pointer position, eased over about 300 to 400 ms (liquidGL-like), and apply the same vector to WebGL rim highlights and to the CSS reflection gradient offset, so metal and glass highlights agree.

### Gaps
- The WICG html-in-canvas explainer's full "privacy-preserving painting" list (visited-link styles, spelling markers, system colors, autofill and so on) was not fetched. Only the cross-origin-iframe exclusion is confirmed.
- No browser vendor position from Mozilla or WebKit was found. Same-origin iframe support and drawable-subtree performance numbers are undocumented in the sources retrieved.
- A Frontend Masters / master.dev hands-on article on HTML-in-Canvas experiments was surfaced but could not be fetched: [Frontend Masters](https://frontendmasters.com/blog/the-web-is-fun-again-first-experiments-with-html-in-canvas/).

## 6. Accessibility and legibility guardrails for hyper-real UIs

### Takeaway
Shipping systems all degrade gracefully. Apple makes glass frostier under Reduce Transparency, black or white with borders under Increase Contrast, and turns off elastic motion under Reduce Motion. Windows swaps Acrylic for solid color under High Contrast, transparency-off and Battery Saver. On the web, `prefers-reduced-transparency` exists but had incomplete support as of its 2023 documentation, so Foresight needs its own user toggle as well as media queries.

### Cited Findings
- Apple applies these automatically when system settings are on. Reduce Transparency "makes Liquid Glass frostier and obscures more of the content behind it". Increase Contrast "makes elements predominantly black or white and highlights them with a contrasting border". Reduce Motion "decreases the intensity of some effects and disables any elastic properties for the material". — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Clear glass without a dimming layer makes legibility "noticeably worse". Avoid content intersecting glass in steady states. — [WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)
- Windows: Acrylic falls back to the user's High Contrast background, and to solid color with transparency off, Battery Saver on, or low-end hardware. Accent-colored text at 14px likely fails contrast on acrylic. — [Microsoft Learn: Acrylic](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/uwp/style/acrylic)
- visionOS: prefer white text on glass, heavier weights (body Medium, titles Bold), system colors calibrated for glass, and avoid thin custom fonts. — [WWDC23 10076](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/)
- CSS `prefers-reduced-transparency` (`reduce` / `no-preference`) maps to Windows, macOS and iOS transparency settings. Support at last update (2023-10-25): Chrome and Edge 118, Firefox behind a flag, Safari not supported. The example raises a frosted layer's alpha to 85% and card backgrounds from 50% to 95% under `reduce`. DevTools Rendering can emulate it. — [Chrome blog: prefers-reduced-transparency](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/css-prefers-reduced-transparency)
- MDN documents a JS `PreferenceManager.reducedTransparency` property (Web Preferences API). — [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/PreferenceManager/reducedTransparency) (surfaced, not fetched; status unverified)

### Inferences
- Guardrail set for Foresight:
  - (a) Never place text directly on refractive or animated glass. Text sits on the tray's content surface (the "screen"), which stays opaque or near-opaque.
  - (b) Measure backdrop luminance under any label that overlays the bench and flip text color, as Liquid Glass does.
  - (c) Honor `prefers-reduced-transparency` (frost more, raise tint alpha to about 85 to 95%), `prefers-contrast: more` (solid fills plus a contrasting rim border), and `prefers-reduced-motion` (disable flex/jiggle, freeze pointer-driven specular travel, keep static highlights).
  - (d) Provide an in-app "material quality" toggle (Full / Acrylic-style / Solid), mirroring Windows' Battery Saver and low-end fallbacks, which also serves integrated-GPU performance.
- WCAG 2.2 thresholds commonly applied here are 4.5:1 for normal text (SC 1.4.3), 3:1 for large text and for non-text UI components and focus indicators (SC 1.4.11). Over textured marble or glass, test contrast against the worst-case (lightest or darkest) backdrop pixel, not the average. These SC numbers are from prior knowledge and the W3C text was not re-fetched this session.
- Apple's "small elements flip light/dark, large ones don't" rule is a good guard against flicker when trays slide over contrasting bench regions.

### Gaps
- Current (2026) Safari and Firefox support for `prefers-reduced-transparency` was not verified. The only fetched source dates from 2023, so the support table may be outdated.
- WCAG 2.2 text was not fetched in this session, and no WCAG guidance specific to translucent or glass backgrounds was found. APCA / WCAG 3 status was not researched.
- No published user-research data on legibility of Liquid Glass-style UIs (for example, post-iOS 26 criticism or Apple's later opacity adjustments) was retrieved. That would be a useful follow-up.
