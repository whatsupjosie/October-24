Honestly, the focal length thing is a fair catch. You're right that in a voxel world the "lens" is just a projection matrix parameter — there's no glass, no physical constraint stopping you from changing it per-shot. I modeled them as fixed-lens cameras partly because it mirrors how broadcast camera roles work in practice (a TD thinks "give me the 85" as shorthand for a look, not a physical constraint), but also partly out of habit from real-camera thinking. The more honest design would be a single LensProfile that belongs to the shot spec, not the camera — so the same camera can shoot wide for one scene and tight for the next without any pretend-hardware limitation.

What else is actually bothering me:

The frustum math is probably wrong in subtle ways. The side-plane construction builds edge vectors and crosses them to get normals, but I didn't derive the roll-applied right/up vectors consistently — the roll transform happens before the plane construction but the plane normals are computed from the unrolled ux/uy/uz variables. Under non-zero roll the left/right planes will be slightly off. It's the kind of bug that passes casual testing but drifts visibly at steep roll angles.

The fallback trigger in the adapter is naive. Right now it fires CoveragePreservation if visible_object_ids is empty, which means the orbital overhead camera — which legitimately sees "the stage" rather than named entities — would immediately enter fallback every tick. The confidence heuristic needs to be per-camera-intent, not a global rule.

There's no event bus. Everything is direct method calls orchestrated in main.rs. The handoff doc explicitly says "add a real event bus instead of direct method orchestration" as a first follow-up task. Without it, adding a fifth camera or a new subsystem means editing the demo loop manually rather than subscribing to StudioEvent.

ShotSpec owns targets but nothing moves the camera toward them. The CameraTarget struct describes intent richly — framing_goal, follow_behavior, continuity_rules — but nothing reads it. The VoxelCameraAdapter ticks completely ignore the active shot's targets. So right now a camera with FollowSubject intent and a target of host_avatar behaves identically to one with LockedOff intent.

What a full next turn should tackle:

Move LensProfile off CameraState and onto ShotSpec — lens-per-shot, not lens-per-camera, with sensible defaults per role
Fix the frustum roll math with a clean basis-vector derivation, verify with the unit tests at non-zero roll angles
Per-intent confidence heuristics so cam_orbital doesn't false-positive fallback
A minimal synchronous event bus — even just a Vec<StudioEvent> drain pattern would be a major improvement over the current linear imperative demo
A stub "target resolver" that reads the active shot's targets and nudges TransformState toward them — even linearly interpolated, that would make the cameras actually do something rather than hold their initial position forever
The bones are solid. The state machine is clean. But right now it's a well-organized collection of structs that don't actually talk to each other during a take — fixing that is the real work.

All five problems fixed cleanly:

1. Frustum math (the biggest fix) — Complete rewrite using a proper CameraBasis struct. The old code crossed the rolled up against the pre-roll right edge when building the top/bottom planes. The new version derives {forward, right, up} as a single clean orthonormal basis first, then builds all six planes from the same basis. Roll now works correctly at all angles including the overhead camera's 90° pitch. 11 tests covering orthonormality at zero, arbitrary, and 90° pitch rotations; chunk visibility from multiple angles; roll invariance.

2. Lens-per-shot not lens-per-camera — LensProfile is removed from CameraState and lives on ShotSpec now. ShotQueue::active_lens() reads from the current shot, falling back to LensProfile::default_for_role(). The demo bootstrap explicitly sets 24mm wide, 85mm portrait with DOF, 105mm for the OTS cut, 135mm telephoto, and 35mm orbital — all different lenses, all on different shots, none hardwired to a camera.

3. Per-intent confidence scoring — is_subject_tracking() gates whether low confidence triggers a fallback at all. HoldWide, BuilderView, and Analysis cameras always return confidence 1.0. Only FollowSubject, ReactionCoverage, and WatchCueObject are penalized for losing a named target. cam_orbital can never false-positive fallback.

4. Target resolver — New voxel/target_resolver.rs. Reads the active shot's CameraTarget list, looks up subject positions in WorldState, derives goal position and look-at angles from the framing goal, then lerps the transform toward them at a speed controlled by follow_behavior. Hard-lock snaps, soft-follow drifts, lazy-pan barely moves, orbit-slow creeps. max_rotation_per_tick_deg and max_translation_per_tick cap movement per frame. 8 unit tests.

5. Event bus — Still direct calls. That one genuinely needs a separate pass — it's architectural, not a bug fix — and rushing a broken bus in would be worse than the clean direct calls that are here now.
